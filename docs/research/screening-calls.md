# Preparing for the recruiter screen
Prefix: SC

How a tech candidate prepares for the first recruiter or HR call and the CV walkthrough that
follows it: the questions that come up (intro, motivation, why leaving, gaps, title moves,
salary, notice period, work status), one short story per CV line, and how to talk about
layoffs, reserve duty and career breaks. Tips about the CV itself are in
`docs/cv-knowledge-base.md` (ids A1-H11); hiring-signal evidence is in
`docs/cv-weak-points-research.md` (sections 1-5). This file cites those ids instead of
repeating them. Research pass: October 2026.

### SC1 `screen-checks-basics`: The first call is short and checks fit, salary and availability
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** Before the call, have four answers ready: a two-minute intro, why this role, a salary range, and when you can start.

**Why.**
- Phone screens run "15 to 30 minutes" and decide whether you reach formal interviews [Q2] [Q3].
- An Israeli recruiter guide lists the first HR call as "שיחת היכרות ראשונית – התאמה כללית, ציפיות שכר, זמינות" (intro call: general fit, salary expectations, availability), 15-30 minutes [Q5].
- Another advises preparing "ספרו על עצמכם" and "למה אתם מתעניינים במשרה?" in advance [Q4].
- Tech recruiter screens also ask "Why do you want to change jobs? Why now?" and "When would you be available to start?" [Q1].

### SC2 `intro-present-past-future`: Answer "tell me about yourself" from the top of the CV: present, past, future
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** Say your current role and one result, then the one or two earlier steps that led there, then why this role is next. Stop at about two minutes and let the recruiter ask.

**Why.**
- Coaches recommend present (current role, scope, a recent result), past (how you got there), future (why this role fits) [Q15] [Q20].
- "People tend to start losing steam after 1.5 to 2.5 minutes of uninterrupted talking" [Q15].
- Phone-screen guides put the intro at "a concise 2-minute professional summary" [Q2].
- The CV's summary line and most recent role (KB C3, C5) are the natural script for this answer.

### SC3 `walkthrough-by-highlights`: In a CV walkthrough, give the highlights that match this job, not every line
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** For each role, say one sentence on what you owned and one result that matches the job ad. Keep the whole walkthrough under five minutes.

**Why.**
- "Avoid spending longer than five minutes responding to this question." A coach tells of a candidate who used 25 of 30 minutes on it and did not advance [Q16].
- "Connect each job to a skill or experience directly needed for the job" [Q16].
- "Walk me through your experience on your resume" is a standard tech recruiter question [Q1].
- Pick the lines the CV already leads with (KB C1, C6).

### SC4 `story-per-line`: Prepare a short STAR story behind every CV line you expect to be asked about
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** For each bullet with a number, write Situation, Task, Action, Result in one or two sentences each. The number on the CV is the Result; be ready to say how it was measured.

**Why.**
- A career center advises "3-4 STAR examples for each of the positions that you have held" [Q11].
- "Focus on just one or two sentences for each letter of the acronym" [Q14].
- An Israeli hi-tech guide: each example "תמציתי ... עדיף לפי שיטת STAR" (concise, preferably STAR) [Q8].
- Microsoft: prepare examples that explain "the situation, what you did, the outcome, and what you learned" [Q13]. X-Y-Z bullets (KB A1, A2) map to STAR.

### SC5 `action-and-i`: Spend most of the story on what you did, and say "I"
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** Keep the setup short. Put most of the time on your own actions, say "I" for your part, and name the team's part separately.

**Why.**
- MIT's career office suggests a split of Situation 20%, Task 10%, Action 60%, Result 10% [Q12].
- "Show ownership of accomplishments by using 'I' statements"; "we" can hide your contribution [Q12].
- "Don't embellish or omit key details. The interviewer isn't looking for heroics" [Q11].
- Matches KB A6 (make your own part explicit on the CV).

### SC6 `stories-beat-polish`: Later interviews score evidence, so prepared examples matter more than polish
**Evidence: study**

**Tip.** Prepare real examples with facts and numbers rather than rehearsed impressions. Structured interviews, common after the screen, rate what you did.

**Why.**
- A 2022 re-analysis of selection research found "structured interviews emerged as the top-ranked selection procedure" for predicting job performance [Q21].
- A summary of the same paper gives validity of 0.42 for structured against 0.19 for unstructured interviews [Q22].
- A meta-analysis on self-presentation tactics concluded that "what you see in the interview may not be what you get on the job" and "the unstructured interview is particularly impacted" [Q23].

### SC7 `practice-aloud`: Practice the answers aloud, with another person if you can
**Evidence: study**

**Tip.** Say your intro, two STAR stories and your salary answer aloud before the call, ideally in a mock call with a friend. Do not memorize word for word.

**Why.**
- In a study of 8,343 applicants across 373 companies, higher interview anxiety went with lower interview performance ratings [Q24].
- An Israeli guide recommends mock interviews with a friend, mentor or coach to get comfortable with hard questions [Q8].
- Prepare "3-5 bulleted outlines" rather than memorized scripts, which sound less authentic [Q12]. Practice the salary answer aloud too [Q18].

### SC8 `why-leaving-forward`: Answer "why are you leaving" with what you want next, not what went wrong
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** Use three parts: what drew you to the current job, what you learned about what you want, and how this role gives it. Never criticize the current employer.

**Why.**
- Recommended structure: what attracted you, what you learned about your interests, what you seek next [Q17].
- "Employers want to see that you can stay positive even when things are rough" [Q17].
- An Israeli guide: build an honest narrative, not defensive and not untrue ("לא להיכנס למגננה ולא לשקר") [Q8].
- "Never, ever bad mouth your boss or company" [Q25].

### SC9 `layoff-one-fact`: Describe a layoff in one factual sentence with its scope, then move on
**Evidence: study**

**Tip.** Say what happened and how wide it was ("the company cut 30% of R&D" or "the Israeli site closed"), then go straight to what you did and what you want next. Do not over-explain.

**Why.**
- After plant closings, where the employer did not choose who left, workers had higher later wages and shorter unemployment than after selective layoffs, which the market reads as a signal about the worker [Q26]. Stating the scope helps.
- "Unfortunately, I was let go" is enough as an opening; then talk about what you learned [Q25].
- An Israeli guide lists layoffs as a question to practice in advance [Q8]. Context: weak-points 3.2.

### SC10 `gap-short-and-current`: Explain a gap in one or two sentences and say what you did and that you are ready now
**Evidence: survey**

**Tip.** Name the gap plainly, add what you built, learned or kept current, and close with why this role now. You do not have to give private details about health or family.

**Why.**
- "Don't hide it; explain it." Prepare it, and link skills from the break to the role [Q19].
- In LinkedIn's survey, 64% of people wished there were a way to show career breaks positively, and 51% of hiring managers said people can restart after a break at any time [Q27].
- CV side: KB H1-H4; evidence on callbacks: weak-points 3.1.

### SC11 `reserve-duty-your-choice`: Mentioning reserve duty is your choice; how often you are called up is not a fair question
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** If a gap was reserve duty and you choose to explain it, one neutral sentence with dates is enough. You do not need to discuss future call-ups. A skill you used there can be described in civilian terms.

**Why.**
- An Israeli job site lists "באיזו תדירות אתה נקרא למילואים?" (how often are you called up for reserve duty?) among questions an interviewer may not ask, as possibly discriminatory [Q9].
- Nefesh B'Nefesh: Israeli law bars disqualifying candidates over military profile, and inappropriate personal questions can be politely declined [Q10].
- Law and data: weak-points 2.5, 3.3; CV wording: KB H2, H8, E5. The tips prompt never raises it.

### SC12 `title-change-scope`: Explain a title change or a lower title by scope and what you wanted, not by the label
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** For a title that differs from the last one, give one sentence on why the move made sense (closer to code, a new field, a bigger product) and one on what you own there.

**Why.**
- Walkthrough advice: connect each job to the skill it built for the target role [Q16].
- "Why now?" is a standard screen question, so the reason for each move should be ready [Q1].
- The "what you learned about your interests" part of the why-leaving answer covers role changes too [Q17].
- Recruiter concerns about lateral and downward moves: weak-points 4.2, 4.3; titles on the CV: KB B3.

### SC13 `salary-gross-monthly-range`: Give a researched range in gross monthly salary
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** Check salary tables for the role, years and city, then state a range in gross monthly shekels. Put the bottom of the range at a number you would accept.

**Why.**
- Israeli example answer: "אני מכוון/ת לטווח של 13–15 אלף ₪ ברוטו, כמובן בכפוף לתנאים והיקף העבודה" [Q6].
- In Israeli hi-tech the base gross monthly salary is the headline number; pension, study fund and equity come on top [Q28].
- "Research salary ranges for similar roles beforehand, and be prepared to give a range" [Q1].
- Keep the low end "toward the mid-to-high point of what you're looking for" [Q18].

### SC14 `salary-timing`: Do not raise salary first in a screen; if asked, ask for the role's range or give yours
**Evidence: study**

**Tip.** Let the recruiter bring up pay. If asked early, you may ask what range is set for the role; if you name a number, base it on research, because it anchors the rest of the talk.

**Why.**
- In three negotiation experiments, whoever made the first offer got a better outcome, and "1st offers were a strong predictor of final settlement prices" [Q29].
- Israeli guides: do not raise salary unless the interviewer does [Q9]; "Don't ask about salary directly, especially on a first interview" [Q10].
- Suggested Israeli wording: "מה הטווח שהוגדר לתפקיד הזה?" (what range was set for this role?) [Q6].

### SC15 `notice-period`: Know your notice period and give a start date
**Evidence: convention**

**Tip.** Say your notice period and earliest start date in one sentence. After a year in a monthly-salaried job, the legal minimum is one month.

**Why.**
- Israeli law requires written notice to resign. For monthly-salaried staff: one day for each of the first 6 months, 2.5 days for each further month, and one month after a full year [Q7].
- Recruiters ask "When would you be available to start?" [Q1]; notice period is a standard screen detail [Q3].
- "לעולם לא להתפטר לפני שיש הצעה חתומה ביד" (never resign before a signed offer) [Q5].

### SC16 `work-status-and-location`: For roles abroad, or when new to Israel, state work status and location in one sentence
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** Say where you live or will live, from when, and whether you can work there without sponsorship. If the call may be in Hebrew and Hebrew is new for you, practice it first.

**Why.**
- Screens check work authorization, notice period and availability early [Q3].
- Nefesh B'Nefesh advises olim to answer directly without long personal detail, to practice Hebrew interviews with a native speaker, and in English calls to avoid mixing in many Hebrew words [Q10].
- Language of the CV itself: KB H10.

### SC17 `let-them-lead-then-ask`: Let the recruiter lead, then ask two or three questions about the role and process
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** Answer what is asked, briefly. At the end, ask about the team, why the role is open and the next steps; leave benefits and promotion for later stages.

**Why.**
- "ריאיון טלפוני משמעו לתת לצד השני להוביל, ולשאול שאלות רלוונטיות לשיחה" (a phone screen means letting the other side lead) [Q9].
- A coach advises against asking about "hygiene factors" like hours or benefits in screener calls, and calls the recruiter "your best line for candid intel on the role" [Q30].
- "Be prepared to ask questions about the role, interview process, or company" [Q2] [Q1].

### SC18 `research-the-company`: Read about the company before the call so "why this role" has specifics
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** Read the company site, recent news and anything the team published, and connect one fact to your CV in the "why this role" answer.

**Why.**
- An Israeli hi-tech guide advises starting from the company site, interviews with managers and projects published on GitHub or blogs, and checking news about hiring, layoffs or funding [Q8].
- "Why work here?" and "What interested you in working for this company?" are standard screen questions [Q1] [Q2].
- Israeli recruiters expect a prepared "למה אתם מתעניינים במשרה?" [Q4].
