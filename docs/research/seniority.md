# Showing seniority and non-standard work
Prefix: SN

How to show seniority and work histories that do not fit one title per employer: staff, principal and lead engineers, engineering managers, architects, founders, freelancers and contractors, part-time and parallel roles, promotions, acquisitions and renames, long tenure, many short contracts, and senior people applying to more junior roles. It builds on the CV knowledge base (KB) and the weak-points research (WP) and cites them instead of repeating: bullet shape and numbers (KB:A1, KB:A2, KB:A6), honest target titles (KB:B3), compressing old roles (KB:C6), job hopping (WP:4.1), and the overqualification and founder studies (WP:4.3).

### SN1 `scope-line-per-role`: Say how far each senior role reached: team, several teams, or the org
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** For each senior role, state the scope in one phrase or the first bullet: whose goals, how many teams, and over what time span.

**Why.**
- Published ladders separate levels by scope. Dropbox IC3: "I own and deliver projects in service of quarterly goals on the team" [Q1].
- Dropbox IC4: "semi-annual/annual goals for my team" [Q7]. IC5 Staff: "multi-year, multi-team product or platform goals" [Q2]. IC6 Principal: "org/company-wide multi-year, multi-team goals" [Q3].
- Tech Interview Handbook lists "Scope of job and skills required" as part of a good accomplishment line [Q15].

### SN2 `scope-not-title`: Do not rely on the title to carry the level; describe the scope
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** Titles mean different things at different companies, so back every senior title with scope and results. Do not inflate the title; see KB:B3 for using the target title honestly.

**Why.**
- "It's hard to compare levels between any two companies, as expectations and responsibilities tend to be defined differently" [Q13].
- Meta engineers at every level have the public title Software Engineer; Netflix has one level, Senior Software Engineer [Q13].
- A senior engineer on an app with thousands of users has "easier to meet" expectations than one on an app with hundreds of millions [Q12].
- About a third of staff engineers in Larson's survey changed companies to get the title [Q11], so the work often runs ahead of the title.

### SN3 `cross-team-influence`: Staff and principal: show influence on teams you did not manage
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** Include at least one bullet where other teams changed what they did because of your work: a roadmap, a shared platform, a standard adopted.

**Why.**
- Dropbox IC5 Staff: "I am increasingly influencing the roadmaps of other Dropbox teams to achieve business impacting goals" [Q2].
- Dropbox IC6 Principal: "I identify and execute on significant group/company-wide opportunities" [Q3].
- StaffEng describes staff projects as "complex and ambiguous", with "numerous and divided stakeholders" and a "named bet where failure matters" [Q10].

### SN4 `staff-evidence`: Write staff bullets like a promotion packet: project, your part, measured impact
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** For staff-level roles, pick the two or three largest projects and give each one line: what it was, what you did, what changed, with a number (KB:A1, KB:A6). Add mentoring and glue work as outcomes, not as duties.

**Why.**
- StaffEng's promo packet asks: "What are your Staff projects? What did you do? What was the project's impact?" [Q9].
- It asks for numbers: "Did you increase revenue by $10 million? Did you reduce year-on-year customer support tickets by 20%?" [Q9].
- It also asks "Who have you mentored and through what accomplishments?" and "What glue work do you do for the organization? What's the impact?" [Q9].

### SN5 `name-the-role-you-played`: When the title hides the role, say which role it was
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** If you worked as a tech lead or architect under a plain "Software Engineer" title, keep the official title and name the role in the first bullet, e.g. "Tech lead for a 6-person payments team".

**Why.**
- StaffEng's four staff archetypes are Tech Lead, Architect, Solver and Right Hand; they describe roles, not titles [Q8].
- "some companies use _Tech Lead_ as a title, and others use it as a role" [Q8].
- Background checks compare employment records with the CV, so keep official names and titles (same logic as SN13) [Q21].

### SN6 `architect-area-and-decisions`: Architects: name the area you owned and the decisions that held
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** Name the technical area you were responsible for, the main decisions you made, and what they produced (cost, reliability, speed of other teams). Avoid a list of diagrams and technologies.

**Why.**
- StaffEng defines the Architect as "responsible for the direction, quality, and approach within a critical area" [Q8].
- "The _Architect_ title has fallen out of style in many companies, but the _Architect_ role remains alive and well" [Q8]. The role needs explaining, not just naming.
- Dropbox IC6 includes a "multi-year, industry-leading perspective, ensuring that our products adapt to scale" [Q3].

### SN7 `em-scope-line`: Managers: open each role with team size, make-up and budget
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** Start each management role with one scope line: number of engineers, which functions, and budget or headcount if you owned it. Then list results.

**Why.**
- Wiz's EM resume guide: "Lead a team of [Number] engineers (Backend, Frontend, DevOps) managing a budget of $[Amount]" [Q14].
- The same guide: team size and budget "anchors your experience in leadership reality before you list specific achievements" [Q14].
- Dropbox M4 expects managers who "can independently plan for the headcount and recruiting needs of my team" [Q4].

### SN8 `em-people-outcomes`: Managers: show hiring, growth and retention as results
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** Give people outcomes with numbers: engineers hired, reports promoted, a team grown from N to M. Pair them with delivery results.

**Why.**
- Dropbox M4 covers hiring ("interviewing and assessing candidates"), retention ("results do not come at the expense of long term growth, retention, or psychological safety"), and delivery [Q4].
- Wiz example bullet: "Mentored [Number] senior engineers to staff-level promotions" [Q14].
- Tech Interview Handbook example: "Led over 5 engineering teams with an average size of 6 members across two companies and mentored over 20 junior members" [Q15].

### SN9 `em-leadership-verbs`: Managers: lead with management impact, not your own code
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** For a manager target, start bullets with what the team achieved under you. Keep hands-on technical work to one bullet, framed as part of leading. For an IC target, reverse the balance (see SN21).

**Why.**
- Wiz: "Use action verbs that signal leadership, such as 'built,' 'scaled,' 'led,' 'partnered,' and 'enabled.' Avoid IC-focused verbs like 'coded,' 'implemented,' or 'debugged'" [Q14].
- StaffEng notes a Tech Lead Manager role "exists on the engineering manager ladder" [Q8]; a hybrid role should say how much of it was managing.

### SN10 `manager-of-managers`: Senior managers and directors: count teams and managers, not just people
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** Say how many teams and how many managers reported to you, the time horizon you planned for, and the business objective you were accountable for.

**Why.**
- Dropbox M5 is "leading through other people managers" and plans "long-term headcount and recruiting needs" [Q5].
- Dropbox M6: "fully accountable for delivering results against a strategic objective directly supporting a company or EPD level business objective over a one to two year time horizon" [Q6].

### SN11 `promotions-under-one-employer`: Show promotions as separate titles under one company heading
**Evidence: convention**

**Tip.** Put the company once, then each title with its own dates, newest first, with bullets under each. The progression is then visible at a glance.

**Why.**
- The Muse gives three formats; separate titles under one heading suit cases where "accomplishments and duties changed significantly after a promotion" [Q18].
- Stacked titles with combined bullets risk ATS software attributing "all your bullet points to your earlier, more junior title" [Q18].
- Cake: "The latest position held should be listed first" [Q20].

### SN12 `promotion-as-evidence`: Tie each promotion to what earned it
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** Where it fits, end the earlier role with the result that led to the promotion, e.g. "Promoted to Senior after leading the migration that cut deploy time by 60%". Use real numbers only (KB:A3).

**Why.**
- Insight Global (a recruiting firm): a promotion "shows that you can handle increasing levels of responsibility" and is "an endorsement of your skills" [Q19].
- It recommends listing the accomplishments that warranted the promotion, with metrics where possible [Q19].

### SN13 `acquisitions-and-renames`: For acquired or renamed employers, show both names
**Evidence: convention**

**Tip.** Use "New Name (formerly Old Name)", or "Old Name (now New Name)" when the old name is better known. Use separate entries only if your role changed with the acquisition.

**Why.**
- "Background and credit checks will turn up those company names, and if your resume doesn't mention them, it sends up a red flag" [Q21].
- Resume Worded lists four formats and suggests separate entries when "you held distinct roles before and after the change" [Q22].
- For a company that shut down while you were there, you can say so to show the exit was not performance-related [Q22].

### SN14 `long-tenure-as-steps`: Split a long tenure into its roles so it reads as change, not repetition
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** Show a long tenure (say 8+ years) as its separate titles or scopes, each with dates. Give the recent ones the bullets and compress the older ones to a line (KB:C6).

**Why.**
- US median tenure with the current employer was 4.1 years in January 2026, and 3.0 years for ages 25-34 [Q29]. A single 10-year entry is far outside that and needs its progression shown.
- Coursera: give each role "different company headers, titles, and years employed in the role" [Q30].
- Cake: listing each promotion "helps reveal your growth capabilities" [Q20].

### SN15 `freelance-one-entry`: Freelancing for a continuous period: one entry, then key clients
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** List continuous freelance work as one entry: a functional title, "Freelance" or your business name as employer, one date range. Name three to five notable clients and results inside it. Give a client its own entry only for a large, long engagement.

**Why.**
- "If you juggled several short-term contracts over a period of time, listing each one separately can make your resume look cluttered" [Q26].
- A continuous date range avoids "the appearance of instability" that fragmented dates create [Q27].
- SelfEmployed.com: include "three to five recognizable names"; pick a title the way "a hiring manager would describe the same role at their company" [Q25].
- Resume Worded: separate entries per client are "great for highlighting big-name clients" [Q23].

### SN16 `confidential-clients`: Describe clients under NDA by sector and size
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** If a client cannot be named, describe it, e.g. "Series C healthcare SaaS company, 500 employees". Drop tiny one-off gigs unless the client is notable.

**Why.**
- SelfEmployed.com: "describe the client by sector and size: 'national healthcare SaaS company (Series C, 500 employees)'" [Q25].
- Resume Worded: "Don't break any NDAs by including them in your resume" [Q23].
- "Very short or one-off projects ... shouldn't be listed, unless they're with a highly prestigious client" [Q23].

### SN17 `many-short-contracts`: Group many short contracts under the agency or under one heading
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** Through an agency: list the agency as employer and the clients under it. Many short contracts of your own: group them in one entry. Only a few: list each and mark it "Contract", so short stints do not read as job hopping (WP:4.1).

**Why.**
- Indeed: "state the staffing company that hired you and then name the individual companies you were engaged with" [Q24].
- Indeed: for a few contracts, add "'temp,' 'temporary,' or 'contract' after the job title" [Q24].
- Tech hiring managers worry about tenure under two years per role; Dice cites "eight months for a new employee to become fully productive" [Q28].

### SN18 `parallel-roles`: List part-time and parallel roles with their real, overlapping dates
**Evidence: practitioner**

**Tip.** Show a side role (part-time job, advisory work, teaching) with its true dates even when they overlap the main job, and mark it "part-time" if that is not obvious. Put the main role first.

**Why.**
- "Listing overlapping dates signals it was part-time or supplementary work, which is perfectly fine" [Q26].
- Harvard's guide says "Avoid information gaps" [Q31]; dropping a real role to keep dates tidy creates a mismatch with background checks [Q21].

### SN19 `founder-commitment`: Founders and ex-founders: show the hands-on work and why you want this job
**Evidence: study**

**Tip.** List the company with an honest title and outcome. Lead with the work the target role needs (code shipped, systems built, team hired), and state in the summary why you want this role now.

**Why.**
- In 2,400 applications to entry-level roles, callbacks were 24% for non-founders, 16.2% for failed founders, 10.9% for successful founders. At mid-level: 13.8% vs 6.3% [Q16]. More in WP:4.3.
- Recruiters feared founders were "too independent, not committed to the company, and unlikely to fit in a traditional workplace structure" [Q16].
- The authors advise founders to "express their desire to fit in and remain committed to the firm" and "show how that experience has set them up for success at that company" [Q16].

### SN20 `failed-startup-is-fine`: A startup that closed is not something to hide
**Evidence: study**

**Tip.** State plainly that the company closed, and write one bullet on what you built or learned. Do not leave the outcome vague.

**Why.**
- Failed founders got more callbacks than successful ones: 16.2% vs 10.9% [Q16].
- Recruiters "acknowledged that most startups fail and value what a candidate learns from such failure" [Q16].
- Noting that a company shut down while you were there shows the exit was not performance-related [Q22].

### SN21 `senior-to-junior-role`: Applying below your level: match the role and answer the commitment question
**Evidence: study**

**Tip.** Keep your real titles, but weight the bullets toward the hands-on work the role needs and compress management scope. Say in the summary why this role and why now (WP:4.3).

**Why.**
- Managers see highly capable candidates as having "lower commitment to the organization than less capable but adequate candidates" and penalise them [Q17].
- The reasons: assumed "lower levels of organizational interest" and more outside options, so higher flight risk [Q17].
- MIT CAPD suggests tailoring by "removing less relevant details or duplicative tasks" [Q32].
